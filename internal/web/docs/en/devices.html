Customers, sites and devices

Devices are organised by customer and site. Technicians and read-only users can be limited to some customers, so every report and every list shows only what they may see.

Customers and sites

  • Customers are the companies you look after (or the departments of your own company). Add them under Customers.
  • Sites are their offices or branches. Every device belongs to one site; enrollment codes are created per site.
  • A customer or site can be deleted only when it no longer contains sites or devices.

The device list

Devices lists all devices you may see with their customer and site, operating system, the signed-in user, whether they are online and when they were last seen. Search by name, user, system or customer, or filter by customer and state.

Filters narrow the list down by customer, state (online, offline), operating system, open alerts, fixable vulnerabilities, policy or a custom field. Technicians choose devices with the boxes in front of them (or all at once) and start an action on all of them: a library script (as a job), searching for and installing the security updates, updating programs with winget, refreshing the inventory or a restart in 5 minutes. Offline devices get the action when they connect; each device's Activity shows how it goes.

The search in the header (Ctrl+K or /) finds devices by name, user, system, serial number, IP or MAC address, customers, installed programs (with the devices that have them) and vulnerabilities by their ID.

The bar with the actions is above the list and shows how many devices are chosen. It also moves the chosen devices to another site, and Remove removes them (online agents uninstall themselves). A device that reports the same hostname as another device you see is marked Same hostname, usually a cloned or reinstalled computer: give it a display name or remove the old one.

Export as CSV downloads the list as it is shown (search, filters or group) for a spreadsheet, with the name, hostname, customer, site, system, agent, state, user and tags of each device.

The device page

A device's page has tabs for everything you can do with it:

Display name and site

Under Name and site on the device page, technicians give a device a display name, for example SERVER-MADRID-01. The console shows it instead of the hostname in lists, search, alerts and reports, with the hostname next to it. The agent never changes the name; an empty name shows the hostname again. A name can also be given at installation, see Installing agents.

Move puts the device into another site, also of another customer you may see. The policies of the new site and customer apply at once; the device keeps its own policy, its tags and fields, and nothing changes on the computer.

Tags and groups

Tags are short names on devices, for example Servers or Reception. Technicians add them on the device page or, for many devices at once, with Add a tag in the device list; the list shows them and filters by them. Scripts can run on the devices of a tag, and administrators can assign a policy to a tag (see Policies). Administrators rename and delete tags under Settings → Tags.

A group is a saved search: search or filter the device list, then Save this search as a group. The group's button above the list shows the devices that match now, so devices that get the tag or the state later appear by themselves. Its creator or an administrator deletes it.

A technician of some customers sees only the tags on devices of those customers, and only the groups saved by administrators, by users of all customers or by themselves: the name of a tag or group can say something about another customer.

Activity

Every action of the console on a device is a task: scripts, library jobs, Windows Update searches and installations, installing and uninstalling programs (winget, Chocolatey, the software library), agent updates, restart and shutdown, also the automatic ones of the server. The Activity tab of a device shows what is running, what is waiting and the history with the result and who started it; the number in the tab counts the running and waiting tasks. A task's name links to its details, for example the output of a script.

The tasks of a device run one after the other. If the device is offline, a task waits until it connects, up to 24 hours. You can leave the page at any time: a quick task shows its result at once, a longer one keeps running and the Activity tab updates by itself. Cancel removes a waiting task; Stop ends a running one: the agent stops the script or installer it runs for it, including the processes they started. Windows itself may still finish the update it is installing at that moment.

Inventory

The agent sends its inventory a few seconds after it connects and then regularly: operating system and build, domain or workgroup, model and serial number, processor, memory, disks with free space and BitLocker state, network adapters with addresses and gateways, Microsoft Defender and its signatures, the Windows Firewall profiles, a pending restart and the installed software (programs on Windows, packages on Linux). Refresh inventory asks for a new one at once.

Restart and shutdown

The power buttons on the device page restart or shut down the computer, now or after a delay, with an optional message to the signed-in user; a scheduled restart can be cancelled. Users signed in there lose unsaved work.

Wake up appears on an offline device whose inventory has a network card address: an online device of the same site (agent 0.14 or newer) sends the Wake-on-LAN packet in its network. Wake-on-LAN must be switched on in the computer's BIOS/UEFI and network card, and it works within one network.

Removing a device

Remove device deletes it from the console. An online agent uninstalls itself; see Removing an agent.
